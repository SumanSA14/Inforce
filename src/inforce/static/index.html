The rule you found may not be the rule that applies

Regulators withdraw instructions constantly, and the withdrawn text stays online — indexed, quotable and indistinguishable from current law. It cannot simply be deleted either: repealed rules still govern conduct from the period when they applied, so an archive that throws them away breaks audit, and one that keeps them undated quietly presents dead law as live.

?

You cannot tell from the document

A withdrawn circular does not say so on its face. Nothing in the text, the title or the URL marks it as dead.

≈

The replacement looks almost identical

A consolidated Direction and the circular it replaced share most of their wording, so they rank next to each other in any search.

An assistant given the wrong source will answer fluently and cite it. Nothing in the reply signals that the ground moved.

How it works

The regulator publishes its own withdrawal list. That list is the ground truth, so correctness here is a matter of record rather than of judgement — and the headline measurement needs no model to grade it.

01

Read the record

Parse the regulator's published withdrawal annex, so every document carries an official in-force or withdrawn label. No hand annotation, and nothing inferred at this stage.

02

Build the archive

Fetch every document, current and withdrawn, and index the full text. Nothing is discarded — the withdrawn material is exactly what you need for a historical question.

03

Give it a sense of time

Record when each document took effect and when it stopped applying, so any date can be reconstructed, and link repealed rules to the ones that replaced them.

04

Measure it honestly

Score it against a question set with published answers, including questions it should refuse. The results — good and bad — are on the Metrics page.

What it does not do

Worth reading before you rely on it. This is a research tool, not a compliance sign-off, and the limitations below are measured rather than assumed.

—

It is not legal advice

It finds and dates source documents. Deciding what they mean for a particular institution is not something it attempts.

—

It can answer when it should refuse

Asked something the archive does not cover, it will sometimes produce a confident answer built from a real figure attached to the wrong obligation. How often is measured and published.

—

It can miss the right document

Retrieval is not perfect, and a source that is never retrieved cannot be reasoned about. Always open the cited document.

—

Replacement chains are inferred

The regulator publishes what was withdrawn, not what replaced it. Successors are inferred from similarity, and that inference can be wrong — particularly where a rule was simply abolished.

Every claim is checked

Answers are generated only from retrieved text, and a figure that appears in none of the sources is withheld rather than shown.

Where the archive does not contain an answer, the intended response is to say so — and how reliably that happens is itself measured, alongside how often it refuses a question it could have answered.

How to use InForce

Four pages. This one explains the other three, what each number means, and — just as importantly — what not to trust.

1 · Explore — ask a question

  1. Type a question, or pick a suggested one. The suggestions are drawn live from questions the naive baseline gets wrong, so they demonstrate the failure rather than flatter the system.
  2. Set the as-of date. This is the whole point. The default is today; set it to before 29 November 2025 and thousands of documents come back to life.
  3. Leave entity scoping on unless you want to see what it does. RBI publishes near-identical rules per entity class, and without it you get the right rule for the wrong kind of bank.
  4. Read both columns. Left is time-blind — what a normal system returns. Right is time-aware. Badges show validity on your chosen date, not today.
  5. Follow the trace. Where the left column returned something repealed, the panel below follows it forward to what governs now.

A search takes a few seconds: it embeds the query, runs two retrievals and walks the supersession graph.

2 · Timeline

Drag the scrubber to any date and watch the corpus change. The cliff between 27 and 29 November 2025 is the natural experiment this project is built on — one notification, thousands of documents invalidated.

3 · Metrics

Every evaluation run ever stored, computed live from results rather than copied in, so the table cannot drift from the database. Click any row for the per-question breakdown.

Reading the numbers honestly

N, or "stale"

How often the top source is a repealed document. This is the headline finding, and it is deterministic — set membership against a published list, with no model grading it.

P@1 strict

How often the one specific document expected to answer the question ranks first. The strictest and least flattering reading. Quote this one.

Recall@8

Whether the right document appears anywhere in the top eight. Much higher than P@1, and much easier — do not confuse them.

Fabrication & false refusal

Always read together. A system that refuses everything scores perfectly on one and is useless. Both are on the home page for that reason.

    Definitions

    P@1 strict — the specific document expected to answer the question is ranked first. The number to quote.

    P@1 lenient — top-1 is in force and the right entity type, but not necessarily the right document.

    Recall@8 / Miss — whether the expected document appears anywhere in the top 8.

    MRR — mean reciprocal rank, scoring 0 when the document is absent. Harsher than MRR@k over a filtered set.

    Stale — top-1 is a repealed document. This is N.

    Wrong entity — top-1 is in force and on the right subject, but for the wrong class of regulated entity (the KYC Directions for NBFCs when Commercial Banks were meant). RBI issues near-identical Directions per entity, so the rule returned is often the same one.

    Wrong subject — top-1 is in force but answers a different question entirely (Capital Adequacy for a securitisation query). These two were one bucket until an adjudication of all 15 cases found 7 were subject misses, which had been inflating the benign-sounding entity mode by roughly 2×.

    —
    Supersession links are inferred, not published — RBI stated which circulars were withdrawn, never where each went. Not legal or regulatory advice.
